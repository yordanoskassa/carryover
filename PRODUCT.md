# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Migrants from East Africa, South Asia, and West Africa seeking legitimate visa pathways to the UK, US, Canada, Europe, and Australia. They encounter fraudulent agencies on Telegram and Facebook promising guaranteed visas. Many are non-native English speakers, often on mobile devices, and in high-stress decision-making situations where financial loss from scams can be devastating. Secondary users: community advocates and diaspora members helping others verify agency claims.

## Product Purpose

ElastiPath protects migrants from visa fraud by providing two core capabilities: (1) an Advisor that surfaces official, government-sourced visa requirements so users can verify claims themselves, and (2) an Inspector that evaluates agency posts against a database of known scam patterns, policy contradictions, and identity reuse signals. A threat intelligence dashboard aggregates scam reports across migration corridors. Success means a user can paste a suspicious Telegram post and get a clear, trustworthy verdict before sending money.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, calm, precise. The interface should feel like a reliable ally, not a surveillance tool. Authority comes from cited government sources and clear evidence chains, not from visual aggression. The tone is protective without being alarmist: present facts clearly, let the evidence speak.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards (Stripe/Linear clones with cold corporate aesthetics that treat users as technical buyers, not vulnerable people making high-stakes decisions)
- Dark-mode-for-cool dashboards with neon accents and crypto-fintech energy
- Government websites that bury critical information under bureaucratic layouts
- Security tools that use fear-based design (flashing red warnings, threat-score theatrics)

## Design Principles

1. **Evidence over opinion.** Every risk assessment shows its reasoning. Never just a score; always the chain of evidence that produced it.
2. **Clarity under stress.** Users are making high-stakes decisions in a second language. Information hierarchy must be obvious. One clear action per screen state.
3. **Calm authority.** Trustworthiness comes from restraint. Neutral surfaces, clear typography, measured color. The interface earns trust by being predictable and readable, not by performing security theater.
4. **Source transparency.** Every piece of advice links back to its government source. The tool is a lens, not an oracle.
5. **Accessible by default.** Low-bandwidth friendly, readable at any text size, functional on budget Android phones. No motion that blocks comprehension.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance. High contrast ratios for all text. Keyboard-navigable throughout. Reduced-motion alternatives for all animations. Consideration for users on low-bandwidth connections and budget devices. Amharic language support planned (already has a toggle stub). No information conveyed by color alone.
